---
title: 'Email'
description: 'Give each teammate an email address of its own, share mailboxes with a project, and read all your mail in one Inbox.'
---

The Email App gives your teammates addresses of their own. A teammate signs up to services, writes to customers
and gets replies, without touching your personal inbox. Every mail your mailboxes receive lands in one **Inbox**,
where you see which teammate has it and approve what it sends.

## Turn it on

Email is off until an owner or admin turns it on: in **Settings → Apps**, open **Email** and choose **Turn on**.
Then give addresses from **Inbox → Mailboxes** (the mailbox icon at the top of the Inbox), or from a teammate's
**Access** tab with **Give an address**.

Every address on Brigade's own domain, `mails.brigade.lol`, sends and receives at once, with nothing to set up.

## Addresses

- **A teammate's address**, such as `riley@mails.brigade.lol`. It belongs to that teammate alone and can't be given
  to another. Names are unique across Brigade and never given to anyone else: turn an address off and on again,
  and the same teammate gets it back. A taken name is offered as `riley-2`, and so on.
- **Project addresses**: in a project's threads, the teammate writes from `riley.<project>@mails.brigade.lol`,
  shown as "Riley · Project". Replies come back to that project. You don't set these up.
- **Sign-up addresses**: a teammate can make a one-off address for a thread, to sign up to a service and receive its
  confirmation code there. The mail goes straight to the thread that made it.
- **Shared mailboxes** belong to a project rather than a teammate, for the addresses customers already write to.
  On Brigade's domain, a shared mailbox gets a generated name such as `support-acme-7f3k`. Point your real address
  at it (see [Your own domain](#your-own-domain)), then choose which teammates read and reply from it.

To add one, choose **Add a mailbox**, pick **An agent** or **A project, shared**, then the address and its domain.

## Where incoming mail goes

Each mail goes to one place, in this order:

1. **A thread waiting for it**: a reply in a conversation the thread started, or mail to its sign-up address.
2. **Its conversation's thread**, if one already has it.
3. **A member's request**: mail from a member of your workspace (checked with DKIM or DMARC) to a teammate's address
   starts or continues a thread with that teammate, as if you had typed it.
4. **The mailbox's rules**, in their order. The first that matches starts a thread with its teammate. A rule starts
   up to 50 threads a day; past that, mail waits in the Inbox and you get a ticket.
5. **The Inbox**, unassigned, for 30 days.

A mailbox's page answers three questions: **Where its mail arrives**, **Who handles it** (its rules, reordered
there by an owner or admin) and **Who reads and replies**. **Send a test mail** checks that the address receives.
A conversation that sends mail to threads 8 times within an hour is stopped as a loop.

## The Inbox

**Inbox**, under Home in the sidebar, lists every conversation your mailboxes received, one row per conversation,
newest first. It also shows Gmail accounts: all mail of a shared account when its menu has **Show all its mail in
the Inbox**, otherwise only mail that went to a thread. A member's own Gmail shows to them alone.

| Tab                | What it holds                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **To do**          | Nobody took it, its teammate chose not to reply, or a reply waits for your approval |
| **With teammates** | A thread has it                                                                     |
| **Scheduled**      | A reply set to leave later                                                          |
| **Done**           | Marked done; the next mail in the conversation waits again                          |
| **All**            | Everything                                                                          |

Filter by source, agent, project or words. From a conversation you can:

- **Hand to…** a teammate, with a note: the conversation then belongs to that thread.
- **Add a rule** (owners and admins) so the next mail like it goes to a teammate.
- When a teammate didn't reply: **Ask Riley to reply**, **Discuss with Riley** (with your teammate's name), or
  **Reply myself**, which sends from the mailbox as you.

## Sending and approvals

A teammate sends from its thread's address: the project address, or the shared mailbox. Mail to anyone outside your
workspace waits for a person to approve it, whatever the teammate's [write policy](/apps#read-write-and-approvals).
Mail only to members of the workspace goes without asking.

The approval card shows who it goes to, the subject, files and text. The member who started the thread, or an
owner or admin, can:

- **Approve & send**: it leaves 30 seconds later and can be cancelled until then.
- **Send later**: pick a time up to 30 days ahead. It shows under **Scheduled** and can be cancelled until it leaves.
- **Edit** the draft first, then send it.
- **Discard** it.

The same card shows in the thread and at the end of the conversation in the Inbox. A teammate can also schedule mail
itself ("send it Monday at 9"), within the same 30 days.

Before anyone is asked, Brigade checks that a reply goes only to the conversation's people (unless the teammate adds
recipients on purpose), that files come from the thread, and that the text holds no secret.

## Replies come back to the thread

When someone replies to mail a teammate sent, the reply wakes the thread that sent it, with the teammate's context.
You don't need to tell it to check. A delivery failure wakes the thread too. If someone already answered from the
mailbox itself, the teammate's own reply after it is refused.

## Your own domain

To send as `support@yourcompany.com`, add your domain in **Inbox → Mailboxes → Domains** with **Add a domain**
(owners and admins). Brigade reads where its mail goes today and asks **How its mail reaches Brigade**:

- **By forwarding**: mail stays with your provider (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365…). You forward each address to
  its mailbox's address on Brigade's domain. The mailbox page says what to forward where, and shows Google's
  confirmation code when it arrives.
- **Directly**: the domain's MX points to Brigade and every address works at once. This is refused while your MX
  points to another provider, since it would take that mail away. Use a subdomain such as `team.yourcompany.com`.

Then add the DNS records Brigade shows at your DNS provider. If the domain's DNS is on Cloudflare and you connected
Cloudflare, **Add them on Cloudflare** adds them for you, without replacing any record. Brigade checks the records
every ten minutes, or at once with **Check now**.

<Warning>
  Add the records within 3 days. After that, while they are not in, another workspace on Brigade can
  add the domain instead. A mailbox on a domain whose records aren't in yet sends nothing: Brigade
  never falls back to its `mails.brigade.lol` address, so replies always come from the address
  people wrote to.
</Warning>

A domain belongs to one workspace. Removing it stops mail going out as it; its mailboxes keep their address on
Brigade's domain.

## Limits

| Limit                        | Value                      |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Mail sent per address        | 100 a day                  |
| Recipients per mail          | 50 (To and Cc)             |
| Files per mail               | 10, up to 25 MB in all     |
| Send later                   | Up to 30 days ahead        |
| Threads a rule starts        | 50 a day                   |
| Unassigned mail in the Inbox | Kept 30 days, then deleted |

Mail a thread has is kept with that thread. Gmail mail you didn't share with the Inbox, and that went to no thread,
is never stored.
